## Section 1 — The audience identity sentence

**The reader is someone reaching for affirmations at a low or hopeful moment — lying awake anxious, trying to rebuild self-worth, wanting steady words over their money worries or their faith, starting a hard week — who is tired of interchangeable lists and of affirmations that feel like lying to themselves, and who wants warm, believable words that meet them exactly where they are.**

Signal (from the corpus + intent, provisional):
- The published library skews to emotionally-charged, need-state queries: anxiety, self-love, confidence, money/manifestation, faith (Bible/Christian), sleep, grief-adjacent (birth/labor, healing), and audience-specific sets (women, men, kids, teens).
- Many queries are time- or moment-anchored ("morning", "Monday", "Friday", "2am" mindset), implying the reader is mid-situation, not browsing.
- A meaningful slice is faith-driven (Bible/Christian/"I am from the Bible") — register must shift to reverent, accurate scripture for those.
- A small novelty slice (funny, sweary, Snoop-Dogg-style) wants playfulness — but still well-formed affirmations underneath.

## Section 2 — The voice rules

1. **Talk like a warm friend, not a guru or a clinician.** Second person, gentle, present. ❌ "The universe conspires to deliver your desires." ✅ "Pick the two or three that feel true today, and carry them with you."

2. **Never fake it — ladder it.** When an affirmation would feel like a lie, offer the believable rung instead of asserting the bold claim. ❌ "I love everything about myself." (for a reader who doesn't) ✅ "I am learning to treat myself with kindness."

3. **Validate the feeling before lifting it.** Name where the reader is, then offer the words. ❌ Opening straight into "You are unstoppable!" ✅ "When your mind won't slow down, these are short enough to hold onto."

4. **Plain words, ~grade 7.** Warm and simple beats elevated. ❌ "cultivate an abundance of efficacious self-regard." ✅ "be a little kinder to yourself."

5. **Honest about what affirmations do.** They're a supportive practice, not magic. Say so without being a downer. ❌ "These will change your life in 21 days." ✅ "Affirmations won't fix everything, but said often and with attention, they can shift how you talk to yourself."

6. **Match the register to the topic.** Faith posts are reverent and scripture-accurate; kids posts are simple and concrete; novelty posts are playful — without breaking the well-formed-affirmation rules. ❌ a jokey aside in a grief/birth post. ✅ a steady, tender tone for the tender topics; a wink for the funny ones.

7. **One clear, kind action at the end.** Point the reader to one sibling set, warmly. No stacked CTAs, no "subscribe/share/comment" pile.

## Section 4 — Touchstone library

Honest reassurances to drop where they fit, and myths to gently correct:

- **Believability ladder** — "You don't have to believe it fully yet. 'I am learning to…' counts." (Corrects the myth that an affirmation only works if you already believe it.)
- **Repetition with attention** — "Saying it slowly, like you mean it, matters more than how many times." (Corrects mindless-repetition / "100x a day" myth.)
- **Support, not a substitute** — "These work best alongside real support, not instead of it." (For clinical topics — always present, never alarmist.)
- **Manifestation = focus + action** — "Affirmations point your attention and motivate the next small step; they don't make things appear." (Corrects the spell myth.)
- **Scripture, spoken in the first person** — "Turning a verse into 'I am…' is a recognized devotional practice." (For faith posts; verse quoted exactly, translation named.)

## Section 5 — Anti-patterns

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| Guru/mystical filler ("the universe is listening", "raise your vibration" as decoration) | The reader came for steady warmth, not a performance; it reads as fake and erodes trust. |
| Denial affirmations for a struggling reader ("I am never anxious", "I feel no pain") | Asks them to lie to themselves; confirms the "these feel fake" fear. Ladder or reframe instead. |
| Outcome promises ("say this and the money/healing/love arrives") | A broken promise to a vulnerable reader is the one failure this site can't afford. |
| Hype stacks ("Powerful! Life-changing! Unlock your highest self!") | Empty emphasis; the reader feels sold to, not met. |
| Toxic-positivity closers ("just choose happiness!") | Dismisses real pain; loses the reader at the most important moment. |

## Section 6 — Whitelist / blacklist

**Cite from:** peer-reviewed psychology + reputable mental-health bodies (APA, NIH/NIMH, university psychology departments) for the science; a reputable Bible reference (always name the translation) for scripture; recognized practitioners (e.g. Louise Hay) attributed *as cultural sources, not scientific authority*.

**Never cite (even when they rank):** content farms promising "manifest your dream life in 24 hours", pages citing studies that don't exist, any source claiming affirmations cure a medical condition or guarantee a windfall, and AI-spun affirmation dumps.
