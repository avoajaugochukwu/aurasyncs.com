faq:
  - q: "What is the solar plexus chakra?"
    a: "The solar plexus chakra, called Manipura in Sanskrit, sits in the upper abdomen just above the navel. In the yogic tradition it is the third chakra and the seat of personal power, confidence, willpower, and self-esteem. Its name means lustrous gem or city of jewels, its element is fire, and its color is yellow."
  - q: "Why is the solar plexus called the third chakra?"
    a: "It is counted as the third of the seven main chakras, moving up from the base of the spine: root (first), sacral (second), then the solar plexus (third). So third chakra affirmations and solar plexus chakra affirmations are simply two names for the same practice — words to support confidence, willpower, and a steadier sense of self."
  - q: "What does a blocked solar plexus chakra feel like?"
    a: "In this tradition, a blocked or low solar plexus chakra is described as low confidence, self-doubt, shame, indecision, or difficulty taking action. An overactive one is described as control, anger, or domineering. These are common human experiences, not a diagnosis — affirmations can support a steadier sense of self but do not treat any physical or medical condition."
  - q: "How do I use solar plexus chakra affirmations?"
    a: "Say them aloud, slowly, with a hand on your upper belly so you feel your own breath move there. Many people use them in morning meditation, before a hard decision, or paired with core-building yoga like boat or warrior pose. Keep the ones that feel believable and repeat them often."
  - q: "Are chakras scientifically proven?"
    a: "No. Chakras come from yogic and tantric spiritual traditions roughly a thousand years old and are a framework for meditation and reflection, not an established medical or scientific concept. These affirmations are a gentle contemplative practice and are not a substitute for medical or psychological care."
