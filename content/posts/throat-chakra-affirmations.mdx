faq:
  - q: "What is the throat chakra?"
    a: "The throat chakra, called Vishuddha in Sanskrit, sits at the base of the throat near the neck and collarbones. In the yogic tradition it is the energy center of communication, truth, and self-expression — how honestly and clearly you can voice your thoughts and feelings. Its name means purification, and its color is blue."
  - q: "How do throat chakra affirmations help with communication?"
    a: "Throat chakra affirmations are short, present-tense lines you repeat to soften the fear of being heard and to encourage honest, clear self-expression. They are self-referential: the act of voicing them is itself the practice. Said often, they help quiet the inner critic that says your voice does not matter."
  - q: "What does it feel like when the throat chakra is blocked?"
    a: "In this tradition, a blocked throat chakra is described as difficulty speaking up, swallowing your words, fear of judgment, talking over others, or holding back what you really feel. These are common human experiences, not a diagnosis — affirmations can support gentler self-expression but do not treat any physical or medical condition."
  - q: "How should I use these affirmations?"
    a: "Speak them aloud, slowly, so your own voice carries them — voicing them is the point. Many people say them in front of a mirror, in meditation, or before a hard conversation. Pair them with free-writing about something you have been holding back to let the words land."
  - q: "Are chakras a proven medical fact?"
    a: "No. Chakras come from yogic and tantric spiritual traditions and are a framework for reflection and meditation, not an established medical or scientific concept. These affirmations are a gentle contemplative practice and are not a substitute for medical, psychological, or speech-related care."
