faq:
  - q: "What is a good example of an affirmation?"
    a: "A good affirmation is a short, present-tense, first-person statement of something you want to be true and can believe — for example, 'I am capable of handling what today brings.' It affirms a direction without denying reality, and it is specific enough to feel personal rather than generic."
  - q: "What are examples of daily affirmations?"
    a: "Examples of daily affirmations include 'I am allowed to take up space,' 'I do good work, and I am still learning,' 'I treat my body with care today,' and 'I am open to receiving good things.' The best daily ones are short enough to remember and tied to whatever you actually need that day."
  - q: "How do I write an affirmation for myself?"
    a: "Start with a worry or a wish, then flip it into a present-tense, positive statement you can believe. If the bold version feels like a lie, ladder it with 'I am learning to' or 'I am open to.' Use your own words and keep it specific to your life."
  - q: "Do affirmations actually work?"
    a: "Affirmations are a supportive practice, not magic. Research on self-affirmation theory suggests that reflecting on what you value can lower stress and defensiveness, but evidence is modest and works best when paired with action and, for clinical concerns, real support."
  - q: "How many affirmations should I use a day?"
    a: "Fewer than you might think. Two or three lines repeated slowly and with attention tend to land better than a long list rushed through. Pick the ones that match your day and carry them with you."
