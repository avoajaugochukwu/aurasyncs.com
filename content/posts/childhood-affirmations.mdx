faq:
  - q: "What are childhood affirmations?"
    a: "Childhood affirmations are gentle, present-tense statements you offer to the younger part of yourself — your inner child. They give you the safety, permission, and love your early years may have lacked, helping you slowly rewrite harsh self-talk that formed long ago."
  - q: "Do inner child affirmations actually work?"
    a: "They are a supportive practice, not a cure. Repeated with attention, kind self-talk can soften the inner critic, and self-compassion research links treating yourself gently to lower stress and self-criticism. For deeper childhood wounds, affirmations work best alongside a therapist, not instead of one."
  - q: "How do I reparent my inner child with affirmations?"
    a: "Speak the words you needed to hear as a child, in the present tense — sometimes to yourself (\"I am safe now\") and sometimes directly to the child within (\"You are allowed to take up space\"). Say them slowly, like you mean them, and pair them with a kind hand on your heart."
  - q: "What if these affirmations feel fake or bring up sadness?"
    a: "That is common, and it is okay. If a line feels untrue, use a gentler rung — \"I am learning to feel safe\" instead of \"I am completely safe.\" If old grief surfaces and feels like too much, that is a sign to be gentle and, if it persists, to reach out to a therapist."
  - q: "How often should I use childhood affirmations?"
    a: "A few minutes a day is plenty. Pick two or three that land, return to them in the morning or before bed, and let them sink in slowly. Consistency and attention matter far more than how many times you repeat them."
