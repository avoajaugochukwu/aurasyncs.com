faq:
  - q: "What are affirmations in simple terms?"
    a: "Affirmations are short, positive statements you say to yourself on purpose — usually in the present tense and first person, like 'I am capable' or 'I am learning to trust myself.' The idea is that the words you repeat to yourself slowly shape how you think and feel about yourself."
  - q: "What is the meaning of positive affirmations?"
    a: "Positive affirmations are phrases meant to gently counter negative or unhelpful self-talk and support a steadier, kinder view of yourself. 'Positive' here means they affirm what you want to build — calm, confidence, self-worth — rather than what you fear."
  - q: "Do affirmations actually work?"
    a: "They can help, but they are not magic. Research on self-affirmation theory suggests that reflecting on what you value can reduce stress and defensiveness. Affirmations work best as a small daily practice paired with action — and alongside real support when you are struggling, not instead of it."
  - q: "What does daily affirmations mean?"
    a: "Daily affirmations simply means making the practice a routine — repeating a few chosen statements each day, often in the morning or before bed. The 'daily' part matters because repetition with attention, over time, is what slowly shifts your inner self-talk."
  - q: "How do I write my own affirmation?"
    a: "Keep it present tense and first person, frame it toward what you want rather than what you fear, and make it believable for where you are today. If a bold line feels like a lie, ladder it: turn 'I am confident' into 'I am learning to trust myself.'"

The meaning of affirmations has always been about influencing your own inner voice, gently and over time, not commanding the outside world."
