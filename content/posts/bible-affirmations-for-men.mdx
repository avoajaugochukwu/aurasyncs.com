faq:
  - q: "What are Bible affirmations for men?"
    a: "They are short, first-person statements that turn Scripture into something you can speak over your own life — for example, taking Joshua 1:9 and declaring 'I am strong and courageous because the Lord is with me.' Turning a verse into 'I am…' is a long-standing devotional practice; the goal is to let God's word, not your circumstances, set the tone of your day."
  - q: "Are biblical affirmations actually in the Bible?"
    a: "The word 'affirmation' is not, but the practice is rooted in Scripture's call to meditate on God's word day and night (Psalm 1:2) and to renew your mind (Romans 12:2). These affirmations are not new truths — they are existing verses, quoted exactly and named by reference, that you speak back to yourself."
  - q: "How should a man use these affirmations day to day?"
    a: "Pick two or three that meet you where you are — before work, in a hard season, or in a moment of fear — and say them slowly, like you mean them. Read the verse first, then the affirmation drawn from it. Consistency over time matters more than intensity on any one day."
  - q: "Do affirmations replace prayer or church?"
    a: "No. Speaking Scripture over yourself supports a life of prayer, community, and worship — it does not replace them. If you are carrying something heavy, lean on your church, trusted friends, and where needed a pastor or counselor. These words work best alongside real support, not instead of it."
  - q: "Which Bible translation are these verses from?"
    a: "Every verse quoted here is from the English Standard Version (ESV), with the book, chapter, and verse named so you can look it up and read it in context. If you prefer another translation, the references make it easy to find the same passage in the version you read."
