faq:
  - q: "What are Bible affirmations for women?"
    a: "They are short, first-person statements drawn from Scripture — turning a verse like Psalm 139:14 into 'I am fearfully and wonderfully made.' Speaking God's word back to yourself this way is a long-standing devotional practice that helps His truth take root in how you see yourself."
  - q: "Are biblical affirmations actually biblical?"
    a: "Speaking and meditating on Scripture is woven all through the Bible — Joshua 1:8 calls believers to keep God's word on their lips and meditate on it day and night, and the Psalms are full of the writer declaring truth over his own soul. Affirmations become biblical when they stay faithful to what the verse actually says rather than reshaping it to fit a wish."
  - q: "How do I use daily Bible affirmations for women?"
    a: "Pick two or three that meet you where you are today. Read the verse first, then speak the affirmation slowly in the morning, write it in a journal beside your prayers, or repeat it before sleep. Let it sit alongside prayer rather than replacing it."
  - q: "What does the Bible say about a woman's worth?"
    a: "Scripture roots a woman's worth in God, not in performance or appearance. Psalm 139:14 says she is fearfully and wonderfully made; Proverbs 31:25 pictures her clothed in strength and dignity; 1 Peter 3:4 calls a gentle and quiet spirit 'of great worth in God's sight.' Her value is given, not earned."
  - q: "Can Christian affirmations replace prayer or church?"
    a: "No. Affirmations are a supportive practice that helps you hold onto Scripture through the day — they work best alongside prayer, community, and worship, not instead of them. If you are walking through something heavy, lean on your church, trusted people, and care as well."
