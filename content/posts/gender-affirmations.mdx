faq:
  - q: "What are gender affirmations?"
    a: "In one sense, gender affirmation is the umbrella term for living as your authentic gendered self — social (name, pronouns, clothing), medical, or legal. On this page, gender affirmations are also short, first-person statements trans and nonbinary people can repeat to steady their self-talk and reinforce a sense of self-worth."
  - q: "Do affirmations actually help trans and nonbinary people?"
    a: "They can support how you talk to yourself, especially alongside community and care. Psychology research has linked social and psychological gender affirmation to lower depression and higher self-esteem among transgender people, though the evidence base is still growing. Affirmations are one gentle part of feeling affirmed — not the whole of it."
  - q: "How do I use these affirmations day to day?"
    a: "Pick the two or three that feel true today and carry them with you. Say them in the mirror, write them in a journal, set one as a phone reminder, or repeat one before a moment you are nervous about. Slow and sincere beats a hundred fast repetitions."
  - q: "What if an affirmation doesn't feel true for me yet?"
    a: "That's okay, and it's common. Reach for the laddered version instead — 'I am learning to…' or 'I am allowed to…' counts. You don't have to believe a line perfectly for it to start softening how you speak to yourself."
  - q: "Can affirmations replace therapy or community support?"
    a: "No. Affirmations support your wellbeing, but they are not a substitute for care or connection. If you are struggling, please reach out to an affirming therapist, a trusted person, or an LGBTQ+ support line — kind words work best alongside real support, not instead of it."
