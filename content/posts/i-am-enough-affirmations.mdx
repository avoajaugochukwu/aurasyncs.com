faq:
  - q: "What does the affirmation 'I am enough' really mean?"
    a: "It means your worth is built-in, not earned. You don't have to achieve, please, or prove anything to deserve care and belonging. 'I am enough' affirms the value you already carry, just as you are right now."
  - q: "What if I don't believe I am enough yet?"
    a: "That's the most common place to start, and it's okay. Use the laddered lines like 'I am learning that I am enough' or 'I am open to believing I am worthy.' These are honest and easier to accept, and belief tends to grow with gentle repetition."
  - q: "How is self-worth different from self-esteem?"
    a: "Self-esteem often rises and falls with how well you're doing. Research by psychologist Kristin Neff found that self-compassion predicts more stable feelings of self-worth than self-esteem, because it's less tied to particular outcomes. 'I am enough' affirmations aim for that steadier ground."
  - q: "How often should I use I am enough affirmations?"
    a: "Pick two or three that feel true today and repeat them slowly in the morning, in the mirror, or any moment self-doubt shows up. Saying a line with attention matters more than how many times you say it."
  - q: "Can affirmations actually build self-worth?"
    a: "They can help. Reflecting on what you value about yourself is part of what psychologists call self-affirmation, which research links to lower stress and defensiveness. Affirmations support that work, especially alongside real reflection and, when self-worth wounds run deep, supportive care."
