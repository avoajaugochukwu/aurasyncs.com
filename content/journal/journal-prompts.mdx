faq:
  - q: "What should I write about in my journal?"
    a: "Start with whatever is loudest in your mind right now, then use a prompt to go one layer deeper. The prompts on this page are grouped by mood — anxious, grateful, stuck, hopeful — so you can pick the set that matches the day instead of staring at a blank page."
  - q: "How do I start a journaling habit that actually sticks?"
    a: "Keep it small enough to feel easy: five minutes, one prompt, no rules about grammar or neatness. Anchor it to something you already do — your morning coffee or the moment before bed — and let consistency matter more than length. A few honest sentences beats a perfect page you never write."
  - q: "How long should I journal each day?"
    a: "There is no required amount. The expressive-writing research often used 15–20 minute sessions, but even five minutes of honest writing is worthwhile. Write until the page feels a little lighter than when you started, then stop."
  - q: "Does journaling actually help, or is it just venting?"
    a: "Both, and the second is part of how it helps. Decades of expressive-writing research by Dr. James Pennebaker and others found that writing honestly about thoughts and feelings is linked to better mood and well-being over time — especially when you move from pure venting toward making sense of the experience."
  - q: "Do I have to journal every day?"
    a: "No. Daily is a nice rhythm, but journaling on the days you need it most still counts. Reach for these prompts when you feel anxious, grateful, stuck, or simply curious about what is going on inside you."
